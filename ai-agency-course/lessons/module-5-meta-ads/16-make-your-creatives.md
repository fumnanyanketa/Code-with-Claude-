## A few plain-language basics first

This lesson uses some everyday terms. Here they are in plain words:

- **Creative:** the actual image or video of the ad — the thing people see.
  Owen: *"Creative is everything."*
- **Aspect ratio:** the shape of the frame. `4:5` is a tall rectangle that fills
  most of a phone feed; `1:1` is a square; `9:16` is full-screen vertical.
- **B-roll:** short background clips (a crew building a deck, a finished roof)
  you lay under a voiceover to keep the eye busy.
- **Voiceover (VO):** the spoken audio read over the video. You can record it or
  generate it with an AI voice.
- **Captions / subtitles:** on-screen text of what is being said. Most feeds
  autoplay muted, so captions are how the message actually lands.
- **Proof:** real evidence your service works — screenshots, reviews, and short
  client videos. Owen: proof that is *"raw over polished… real, raw, or recent"*
  wins.
- **Testimonial:** a client saying, in their own words, what changed for them.

You do not need to memorise these. Each is explained again the first time it matters.

## Why this lesson matters

You already have a script — that was Lesson 15. But a script in a doc gets you
nothing; a homeowner has to *see* something. This lesson is where the script
becomes an asset that runs. And it is the highest-leverage work you do, because,
as Owen says, *"one ad, literally just one winning ad, can completely change

targeting, your landing page, your copy — literally none of it matters if your
creative doesn't make someone stop scrolling."* AI now lets a beginner make a
week's worth of these in an afternoon.

## Part 1: Filming a video ad on a phone (script → footage)

You do not need Owen's studio. He is honest that his own rig — an Aputure light,
a Sony FX3, a Shure SM7B mic, *"about 3ish K total… kind of the guru setup"* — is
optional. The bootstrapped version is a phone, a tripod, and a teleprompter app.

**The minimum kit:**

| Piece | Bootstrapped version | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Your phone, recording **native to the platform** (vertical) | Feeds are vertical; shoot vertical |
| Stability | A simple phone tripod | Shaky footage reads as amateur |
| Teleprompter | A teleprompter app that records in 4K while text scrolls | You read the script and look at the lens |
| Audio | A clip-on mic that plugs into the phone | *"Having good audio is very important… you don't want just the basic audio"* |

> 🔑 **Good audio and a steady, well-lit phone shot beat an expensive camera with
> bad sound. Script and message matter more than gear.**

### Location congruency — the mistake beginners make

This is the load-bearing idea of Part 1. Where you film changes results:

> ✅ **Film where your audience is comfortable, not where you think looks
> impressive.** Owen: *"Do not take some extremely expensive high-end studio and
> expect contractors to resonate with that… You think it makes you look higher
> authority because it looks good to you, but it looks good to you because that's
> what you know. They don't know it. It doesn't look good to them."*

The rule of thumb:

- **Client (B2C) ads** — homeowners hiring a contractor — film in a
  neighborhood, on a job site, or somewhere familiar and everyday.
- **Agency (B2B) ads aimed at founders doing $1M+/yr** — a clean, professional
  setup makes sense there.

A mismatched, over-produced studio gets you a *lower* click-through rate.
Congruency, not polish, is the goal.

### Recording tips Owen actually uses

- **Look your best.** *"People judge you in a split second."* Shower, tidy the
  background, feel good.
- **Test before you film.** Take 15 minutes to check audio and camera. A ruined
  hour of footage is avoidable.
- **Commit to finishing each take.** Your first videos will *feel* terrible —
  *"this is completely normal."* You control the final cut; re-record tomorrow if
  you hate it.
- **Lighten up and smile.** Chasing perfection makes you look stressed on camera.

### Editing

Owen's best ads have *"captions, subtitles, B-roll, motion graphics… background
music, sound effects,"* but he warns: *"please don't over complicate this…
they just have to be engaging and make sense."* He mainly uses **DaVinci
Resolve**; the source also uses **CapCut** for its transitions and sound library.

> 💡 **Editing tool alternatives.** CapCut (free, easy) and **DaVinci Resolve**
> (free, powerful) are the obvious starting points. Others: **Adobe Premiere**,
> **Final Cut**, and **Descript** (which edits video by editing the transcript —
> great for cutting filler words). Any of them can add B-roll, captions, and
> music; pick one and stop shopping.

## Part 2: AI picture ads (the fastest path for a beginner)

Owen's strongest recommendation for anyone new: **start with pictures.** *"Picture
creatives are by far the most scalable and simple method… they're so easy, high
performing."* One picture ad, he says, drove *"900 plus leads… $20,000 a month in
cash collected."*

The workflow has three moves, and the key one is that **you don't write the image
prompt yourself — you have an AI assistant write it.**

```text
1. FIND A WINNER   → Facebook Ads Library, filter to ads running 4+ months.
                     A long-running ad is almost always a winner.
2. PROMPT WITH AI  → Ask Claude to WRITE the image prompt, describing the
                     winner's style + your offer. ("Write me a Higgsfield
                     prompt for a winning ad for roofers, white creative,
                     headline 'Roofers', line under it 'We'll get you 6–8
                     jobs on a pay-per-result basis'… make it cohesive and
                     high-performing.")
3. GENERATE        → Paste that prompt into your image tool. Set aspect
                     ratio to 4:5 (native to the feed). Use the winning ad
                     as a visual REFERENCE, then tweak location to match
                     your audience.
```

Owen's exact habit: *"I like to use Claude to make good Higgsfield prompts."* And
on aspect ratio: *"it's actually better to do 4 to 5… because it's more native to
the platform."*

**Two picture styles that work in home improvement:**

1. **Price-anchor style.** A clean creative with a headline callout ("Roofers")
   and a price-anchored offer ("New deck starting at $9,500"). The price is the
   differentiator.
2. **Authentic-selfie style.** *"The authentic selfie has been very high
   performing for me."* A real-looking person, an everyday setting, the offer on
   a sign or overlay. A common winner: a small family holding a yard sign with
   the price, plus a few photos of finished jobs.

> ✅ **Use a proven winner as your reference, match the location to your
> audience, export at 4:5, and let Claude do the prompt-writing.** That is the
> entire loop, and Owen makes each ad in *"less than 5 minutes."*

> ⚠️ **Reality check — disclose AI imagery honestly, and never pass off
> AI-generated or stock photos as a specific client's real work.** AI selfies and
> AI job photos are fine as *illustrative* creative, but the moment an ad implies
> "this is a real family we served" or "these are this contractor's actual
> completed jobs" when they are not, you have crossed into a deceptive,
> potentially false-advertising claim. FTC endorsement and advertising rules
> require claims and depictions to be truthful and not misleading. If a photo
> looks like a real customer or a real result, it must **be** one. When in doubt,
> use clearly generic imagery and keep the specific, verifiable proof (real
> reviews, real numbers) for the claims — that is what Part 4 is for.

## Part 3: An AI video ad — B-roll + voiceover + captions

When you don't want to be on camera, you can build a video ad entirely from AI
parts. This is exactly what Owen does live in the source for a decking client
with a *"$9,500 near Hampton Roads"* price-anchor offer.

```text
SCRIPT (from Lesson 15)
   │
   ├─► B-ROLL   Generate 5-second clips in an AI video tool (vertical 9:16):
   │            a crew building, materials going in, a finished deck, the
   │            client's local area. Also mine Facebook for your niche —
   │            sped-up build videos with lots of likes tell you what reaches.
   │
   ├─► VOICEOVER Paste the script into an AI voice tool, audition voices,
   │            pick one that fits (Owen chose a warm, natural read),
   │            generate the VO.
   │
   ├─► ASSEMBLE  In your editor: lay B-roll in order, drop the VO on top,
   │            trim/split the audio clean, add subtle transitions + a bit
   │            of music and a light sound effect. Don't overdo it.
   │
   └─► CAPTIONS  Add big, legible, "glowy"/noticeable captions. Size them up
                and position them mid-lower so nothing important is covered.
                Most viewers watch muted — captions carry the message.
```

Owen's real example ran short on purpose — *"these are going to be somewhat
shorter ads because we don't really need them to be too long."* Short, punchy,
captioned, price-anchored.

> 🔑 **The three ingredients of an AI video ad are B-roll, a voiceover, and
> captions. Captions are not optional — muted autoplay means unreadable captions
> equal no message.**

> 💡 **Tool alternatives for AI video.** The source uses **Higgsfield** for
> B-roll generation, **ElevenLabs** for the voiceover, and **CapCut** for
> assembly. Real substitutes:
> - **AI video / B-roll:** Runway, Kling, Pika, Google Veo, Sora, or Canva's
>   video generator.
> - **AI voice:** PlayHT, Murf, WellSaid, or OpenAI TTS instead of ElevenLabs.
> - **Editing/captions:** DaVinci Resolve, Adobe Premiere, or Descript instead
>   of CapCut.
>
> None of these lock you in. Pick one of each and produce.

### A note on hosting

If you later host a sales/VSL video on a landing page rather than in the ad
itself, Owen uses **Vidalytics** for hosting and view analytics.

> 💡 **Hosting alternatives to Vidalytics:** Wistia, Vimeo, VdoCipher, or Gumlet.
> For an in-feed ad you upload straight to Meta — you only need a host for videos
> embedded on your own funnel pages.

## Part 4: The proof-capturing SOP

Proof is the fuel for every future creative. Owen: *"Proof is extremely important
when it comes to marketing and overall growth."* The whole system is a habit plus
a folder.

**What makes proof stronger** (Owen's rules):

- Raw **over** polished.
- Live **over** recorded.
- **Showing** instead of telling.
- **Many** mini-testimonials over one big glossy one — *"people trust people that
  look like them."*
- Third-party (a real review) over self-proclaimed.
- Real, raw, or recent **wins**.

**The system:**

1. **Make a proof vault.** A single Google Drive folder you add to forever. It
   *"will always give you new creative ideas and differentiate yourself from
   competitors."*
2. **Book a weekly 30-minute block.** Harvest everything: video testimonials,
   screenshots of leads / booked appointments / closed jobs, and text wins ("I
   got my first lead!") from chat or Slack.
3. **Record the starting line.** At the start of a client relationship, capture
   where they are now — jobs per month, average revenue, what isn't working — in
   a quick selfie-style video. Later wins mean nothing without a "before."
4. **Capture at every milestone, immediately.** Do not wait for a life-changing
   result. First appointment, first job, first profitable month — *"hey, can you
   grab your phone? Just record a quick 30 second win."* Catch them on the
   emotional high. Over time you build proof of the whole client journey.

**Testimonial questions to ask** (Owen's set):

- What happened before you joined?
- What made you join?
- What happened after — what results did you see?
- How do you feel now?
- Who would you recommend this to?

Then reuse that proof *"everywhere"* — ads, emails, landing pages, pre-call
videos, onboarding.

> ⚠️ **Reality check — proof must be REAL and substantiated. Never fabricate a
> result or use AI to invent a testimonial or a client.** This is the single
> hardest line in the lesson. FTC endorsement rules require testimonials to
> reflect the honest experience of real people, and require any results claim to
> be truthful, substantiated, and not misleading (a result you *can* prove is
> typical, not a lucky outlier dressed up as normal). Generating a fake
> "client," writing a testimonial the client never said, or inflating "$30k/month
> in 2 months" numbers is deceptive and can be illegal — and it destroys the
> local reputation this business depends on. The proof vault exists precisely so
> you never have to make anything up: capture enough real wins and you will
> always have the truth to show.

> 🔑 **A weekly 30-minute proof habit, capturing real wins at every milestone, is
> what turns one client's genuine result into your next winning ad.**

## Key takeaways

1. **Creative is everything.** Owen: *"Your targeting, your landing page, your
   copy — none of it matters if your creative doesn't make someone stop
   scrolling."* This is your highest-leverage work.
2. **Film where the audience is comfortable.** Congruent location beats an
   expensive studio; a mismatched studio *lowers* your click-through rate.
3. **Picture ads first.** Fastest, cheapest, highest-performing for beginners.
   Have Claude write the prompt from a proven winner; export at 4:5.
4. **AI video = B-roll + voiceover + legible captions.** Short and captioned,
   because feeds autoplay muted.
5. **Proof is fuel, and it must be real.** A weekly vault habit and
   milestone-capture give you truthful evidence for every future ad.

## Common pitfalls

- ❌ **Renting a fancy studio to look "high authority" for contractor clients.**
  It reads as foreign to them and drops your CTR. Film in a familiar, everyday
  location.
- ❌ **Ignoring audio.** Great visuals with tinny phone audio kill the ad. Use a
  clip-on mic and test it first.
- ❌ **Tiny or unreadable captions.** Muted autoplay means unreadable captions =
  no message. Make them big and well-positioned.
- ❌ **Exporting the wrong aspect ratio.** A square where the feed wants 4:5, or
  horizontal where it wants vertical, gets throttled. Match the placement.
- ❌ **Implying AI imagery is a real customer or a client's real job.** Disclose
  AI creative honestly; keep specific "this really happened" claims tied to real,
  verifiable proof.
- ❌ **Fabricating or exaggerating testimonials and results.** It is deceptive,
  can violate FTC rules, and wrecks your reputation. Capture real proof instead.
- ❌ **Waiting for a "huge" win before asking for a testimonial.** Capture the
  small milestones immediately, on the emotional high.

## 🛠️ Capstone Project: A real ad batch for your niche

> This is the main hands-on project for the lesson. When you finish, you have
> assets you could actually launch tomorrow — and a habit that keeps them
> improving.

### What you will build

A launch-ready creative batch for **your** niche, built from your Lesson 15
script: **at least two picture ads and one short video ad**, all exported at the
correct aspect ratio with legible captions and an offer that matches your funnel.
Plus your **proof-capture folder** and a **weekly 30-minute habit** on the
calendar. Each piece maps to a lesson idea:

- Picture ads (price-anchor + authentic-selfie) → Part 2
- Short video ad (B-roll + VO + captions) → Parts 1 and 3
- Proof folder + weekly habit → Part 4
- Honest, real, substantiated claims throughout → the Reality checks

### Why this is the perfect practice

| Lesson idea | Where you use it in the batch |
|---|---|
| Location congruency | Choosing the setting for your video and selfie ads |
| Claude-written image prompts | Generating both picture ads from a proven winner |
| 4:5 / vertical aspect ratios | Exporting every asset for the feed |
| B-roll + VO + captions | Assembling the video ad |
| Proof SOP | Standing up the vault and the weekly block |
| Truthful-claims rule | Keeping every ad honest and defensible |

### Milestones (build them in order, each one works on its own)

1. **Pull two winners.** In the Facebook Ads Library, filter your niche to ads
   running 4+ months and save two you want to emulate — one price-anchor style,
   one authentic-selfie style.
2. **Make picture ad #1 (price-anchor).** Have Claude write the image prompt from
   winner #1 plus your offer; generate it in your image tool at **4:5**; set the
   location to match your audience.
3. **Make picture ad #2 (authentic-selfie).** Same loop with winner #2. Export
   at the correct ratio.
4. **Produce the video ad.** Generate 3–5 vertical B-roll clips, create the
   voiceover from your Lesson 15 script, assemble in your editor with light
   music/transitions, and add **big, legible captions**.
5. **Match the offer to the funnel.** Confirm the price/offer shown in all three
   creatives is identical to what your landing page or lead form says. Congruency
   is non-negotiable.
6. **Stand up the proof vault.** Create the Google Drive folder, add any real
   proof you already have, and drop in the five testimonial questions as a note.
7. **Stretch goals.** Book a recurring 30-minute weekly "proof harvest" block on
   your calendar; make a filming/selfie version of one picture-ad concept; write
   one copy variant with Claude (callout → pain → offer/benefits → CTA).

### How you will know you are done

- ✅ Three assets exist: two picture ads and one video ad, in your niche.
- ✅ Every asset is exported at the **correct aspect ratio** (4:5 or vertical
   9:16) — no wrong-shape frames.
- ✅ Captions on the video are **legible** at phone size (big, well-positioned,
   readable muted).
- ✅ The price/offer in every creative **matches the funnel** exactly.
- ✅ A proof-capture folder exists and a weekly 30-minute block is on the
   calendar.
- ✅ Nothing in the batch is fabricated: any depicted "customer/result" is either
   clearly generic AI creative or backed by real, verifiable proof.

> 💡 **Keep yourself honest:** if you could not defend a single claim or image in
> front of the client it depicts, it does not ship. Real proof, real name, real
> numbers, or clearly generic — nothing in between.

## Practice exercises (optional extra reps)

> **What these are:** small, self-contained tasks, each giving focused practice
> on one idea. Optional and independent; the Capstone already touches all of
> them, so feel free to skip straight to it.

### Exercise 1: Prompt-from-a-winner (foundational)
Pick one long-running ad from the Ads Library. Ask Claude to write an image
prompt that recreates its *style* with your offer, then generate it at 4:5.
Practices Part 2 — letting the AI write the prompt.

### Exercise 2: Caption legibility test (intermediate)
Take any 15-second clip, add captions, export it, and watch it **muted** on your
phone at arm's length. If you can't read every word, resize and reposition until
you can. Practices the caption discipline from Part 3.

### Exercise 3: Milestone capture script (advanced)
Write the exact 3-line message you'll send a client the moment they hit their
first booked appointment, asking for a 30-second video win — warm, specific, easy
to say yes to. Practices the proof SOP from Part 4 before you need it live.

```text

  Questions: before? · why join? · after/results? · feel now? · recommend to?
```
