A video sales letter (VSL) is a short recorded pitch that fills a cold prospect in on who you are and what you deliver so they book a call, and a properly warmed-up Facebook and Instagram page — one you nudge with a tiny $5/day engagement campaign — lowers the CPMs your future ads will pay.

## A few plain-language basics first

This lesson uses some everyday terms. Here they are in plain words:

- **VSL (video sales letter):** a short video, often just you talking to a phone camera, that does the selling — it warms a stranger up enough to book a call. Owen calls it a "VSSL."
- **Homepage VSL:** the VSL that plays on your landing page, before anyone has booked. It has to earn a click from a cold visitor.
- **Pre-call VSL:** a second, more personal VSL that plays *after* someone books, to warm them up before the call so they show up ready.
- **CPM:** "cost per mille" — what Meta charges you per 1,000 times your ad is shown. Lower CPM = cheaper reach = more results for the same budget.
- **Engagement campaign:** a Facebook ad campaign whose goal is likes and follows, not leads — used here purely to "warm up" a new page.
- **Ad account restriction:** Meta freezing or limiting your ability to run ads, often triggered by new accounts doing too much too fast.
- **Embed code:** a snippet of code your video host gives you so the video plays inside your own web page.

You do not need to memorise these. Each is explained again the first time it matters.

## Why this lesson matters

Your ad gets the click. But a cold contractor who lands on your page has no idea who you are, whether you're real, or why they should trust a 20-year-old with their marketing budget. The VSL is what fills that gap. As Owen puts it, the whole reason he built one is *"people don't have enough information about who I am, what I do, and proof. So I like to fill them in with this information here so they know more about me to apply or book a call."* And the page warm-up is the quiet back-end work that makes every future ad cheaper: *"getting this stuff set up on the back end can definitely help lower your CPMs and get you better cost, better results for your ads in general."*

## Part 1: What a VSL is, and the two kinds you need

A VSL is a short video that does your selling for you. For a cold visitor who has never heard of you, a wall of text rarely lands — a person talking to camera, with real proof on screen, does. Owen runs **two** of them:

| | **Homepage VSL** | **Pre-call VSL** |
|---|---|---|
| Plays | On your landing page, before booking | After someone books, to prep them |
| Audience | Cold — never heard of you | Warm — already raised their hand |
| Job | Earn the click / the booking | Reduce no-shows, build trust, add proof |
| Style | Punchy, benefit-led | More personal, more proof-heavy, more detail |

The pre-call VSL, Owen says, *"is usually a little bit different. It's usually more proof oriented, more personal, and has a little bit more information about like what you do."* In his own funnel he put so much into the homepage VSL that he deliberately made the pre-call one *"a little bit shorter and simpler."* The point isn't a fixed rule — it's that the two videos have different jobs, so they say different things.

> 🔑 **A VSL is direct-response copy on video: hook the right person, name their pain, show the mechanism, prove it, ask for the booking.**

## Part 2: The "verify me, don't trust me" angle

The most powerful move in Owen's homepage VSL is that he never asks for trust. He hands over proof the prospect can check themselves:

> *"Before I say anything else, I want to show you three real contractors with real numbers. And yes, you can call every single one of them yourself... I'm not asking you to trust me. I'm asking you to verify me."* — Owen (VSL script)

Paired with this is the **pay-per-show, risk-reversal** framing: *"you literally only pay when you sit down with a homeowner at the kitchen table... I literally take all of the risk."* Because he only gets paid when a real homeowner shows up, his incentive is identical to the client's — and he says so out loud. That alignment *is* the pitch.

> ⚠️ **Reality check — proof must be real.** "Verify me, don't trust me" only works if the numbers survive verification. Under the **FTC's endorsement and testimonial rules**, results and testimonials in your VSL must be truthful, substantiated, and not misleading, and income claims must reflect what a typical client can actually expect — "$30k/month" is not typical. If you're borrowing proof from your mentor's results while you get your own, say so honestly; never present someone else's wins, or invented ones, as your own client outcomes. A VSL that invites people to call your references is exactly the wrong place to have fabricated references.

## Part 3: The production workflow

The whole VSL is cheaper and simpler to make than beginners expect. Owen's pipeline:

```text
1. SCRIPT     Start from a proven script → tailor with AI (Claude/ChatGPT)
                 └─ swap in YOUR real proof, YOUR niche, YOUR voice
2. RECORD     Phone camera, clean background (not your bed), just talk to it
3. EDIT       Cut the dead air, add a subtle zoom, splice in proof screenshots
4. CAPTION    Auto-generate captions so it's watchable on mute
5. HOST       Upload to a video host → grab the embed code → drop into your page
```

**Script.** Owen starts from a working script and adapts it: *"Then once you actually have the script you can just talk to Claude on making it better... that's what I did."* Change the proof — it isn't yours — and make it your own.

**Record.** *"You can record it with your phone in any kind of environment. Super simple."* Just get a clean background.

**Edit.** He uses DaVinci Resolve — cutting with a splice, removing sections he doesn't want, adding a gentle dynamic zoom, and dropping in proof screenshots his page builder generated.

**Caption.** He exports the edit into CapCut, hits **captions → generate**, picks a template, and downloads. *"It truly doesn't matter, just as long as it's engaging in some way."*

**Host.** He uploads to Vidalytics, which gives play-rate analytics and things like a "rapid engage" seek bar that makes the video feel shorter than it is. Then he copies the **embed code** and drops it into his landing page. Aim for a play rate and engagement of at least ~20%; a video thumbnail, a more curious headline, and visual aids (a slideshow, clean captions) all lift it.

> 💡 **Tool alternatives (don't get locked in).**
> - **Hosting:** Owen uses **Vidalytics**. Substitutes — Wistia, Vimeo, VdoCipher, Gumlet.
> - **Editing:** he uses DaVinci Resolve for the cut and **CapCut** for captions. Both DaVinci Resolve (free) and alternatives like Adobe Premiere, Descript, or Final Cut can do the whole job start to finish.
> - **AI voice (optional):** if you'd rather not use your own voice on a slideshow-style pre-call VSL, tools like **ElevenLabs** — or PlayHT, Murf, WellSaid, OpenAI TTS — can narrate it. (Your own face and voice usually convert better, though.)

> ✅ **What to do about it:** ship a rough V1 today. Owen: *"Do as best as you can on camera. I know it's probably new for some of you. That's just fine."* A live, imperfect VSL beats a perfect one that never gets recorded.

## Part 4: Setting up your Facebook & Instagram pages

Before you can run ads, you need a **business page** (not just a personal account). Go to `facebook.com/pages` and create a new page off your account. Two decisions matter:

**Personal name vs company page — decided by niche.** Owen runs his page under his own first and last name, with a photo of himself:

> *"They'll basically trust you more because instead of being a giant company... you're just a one-man band. And honestly, for some reason, contractors tend to trust that more because they want to work with a person and not a company."*

But he's explicit that this is niche-dependent: *"If you're working in the medical space... you probably want to be seen as a bigger company, so you probably don't want to do this."* Home improvement → a personal name works. Higher-trust or corporate niches → a branded company page. You can set up both.

**Don't spin up an ad account immediately.** This is the easy-to-miss trap:

> *"Be careful that you don't create an ad account right after you make this page... because they'll get restricted if you make things too quickly."* — Owen

New assets doing a lot, fast, look suspicious to Meta. Create the page, let it settle, add a profile photo, cover art, and a couple of posts before you attach an ad account or business portfolio.

> 🔑 **Create the page like a real person would, then slow down before wiring up ads — speed is what gets new accounts restricted.**

## Part 5: The $5/day engagement warm-up campaign

A brand-new page with no followers gets pushed less and pays higher CPMs. The fix is a tiny **engagement campaign** whose only goal is likes:

```text
CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE   Engagement (NOT leads / awareness)
OPTIMIZE FOR         Maximize page likes
BUDGET               ~$5 / day per ad set (ad-set budget)
PLACEMENT / CONV.    Facebook + Instagram, pointed at the page you're warming
TARGETING            Your contractor-age band (~29–64), your geo
CREATIVE             One simple image — a friendly, relevant post
```

Owen runs one of these per page — personal, B2C, and B2B — at *"$5 a day in each ad set. And that right there will get you hundreds of followers."* His targeting age band (~29–64) matches the contractors and business owners he actually wants engaging with the page. Keep the geo relevant to the market you serve.

Owen is refreshingly honest about how much this matters: *"this stuff is not that important... At the end of the day, the ads are going to do the most needle-moving work."* The warm-up is a cheap edge, not the main event — worth doing, not worth obsessing over.

> ⚠️ **Reality check — do NOT buy cheap likes from far-off geos.** Owen mentions that to get *"a ridiculous amount"* of followers you could target Colombia or Pakistan, where likes are far cheaper. **Skip this.** Buying engagement from unrelated cheap-labor geographies is against **Meta's terms** (it's a form of inauthentic engagement), and it's pure **vanity** — followers who will never become clients, don't match your audience, and can actually *muddy* the signal Meta uses to find real buyers. Warm up with **genuine, relevant engagement** from the audience you actually serve. A page with 200 real, on-target followers beats one with 5,000 irrelevant ones every time — and it won't get your account flagged.

## Key takeaways

1. **A VSL fills the trust gap your ad can't.** It tells a cold prospect who you are, what you do, and shows proof — so they book.
2. **Two VSLs, two jobs.** Homepage VSL earns the click; the more personal pre-call VSL reduces no-shows and adds proof.
3. **"Verify me, don't trust me."** Hand over checkable proof and align incentives with pay-per-show — but the proof must be real (FTC).
4. **Production is cheap.** Script with AI, record on a phone, edit + caption, host, embed. Ship a rough V1.
5. **Page name follows the niche.** Personal name for home improvement; company page for higher-trust niches.
6. **Don't rush the ad account** — that's what gets new pages restricted. Then warm up at ~$5/day for page likes, targeting your real audience.

## Common pitfalls

- ❌ **Waiting for a "perfect" VSL.** A live, rough VSL beats a polished one that never records. Do V1 today.
- ❌ **Fabricated or borrowed proof presented as your own.** Illegal under FTC rules and fatal in a "call my references" pitch. Keep it truthful.
- ❌ **Creating an ad account seconds after the page.** Too much, too fast → restriction. Let the page settle first.
- ❌ **Buying cheap likes from Pakistan/Colombia.** Against Meta's terms, pure vanity, and it pollutes your targeting signal.
- ❌ **A homepage VSL with no proof and no clear ask.** Cold traffic needs proof + one obvious next step (book a call).

## 🛠️ Capstone Project: Ship a VSL and a warmed-up page

> This is the main hands-on project for the lesson. You will end with a real, embeddable VSL, a live Facebook + Instagram page, and a small warm-up campaign running — the front-end assets your Module 5 ads will point at.

### What you will build

A rough-but-real homepage VSL hosted and embedded on your funnel; a published Facebook business page and matching Instagram page; and a compliant $5/day engagement campaign warming that page up. Each piece maps to a lesson idea.

### Why this is the perfect practice

| Lesson idea | Where you use it |
|---|---|
| Two kinds of VSL | You script and record at least the homepage VSL |
| Verify-me / honest proof | Your VSL uses only real, checkable claims |
| Production workflow | Script → record → edit → caption → host → embed |
| Page name by niche | You choose personal vs company for your niche |
| Don't rush the ad account | You create the page, then wait before attaching ads |
| Compliant warm-up | $5/day, page likes, your real audience — no bought geos |

### Milestones (build them in order, each one works on its own)

1. **Draft the script.** Start from a proven VSL script, then use Claude/ChatGPT to tailor it to your niche and your (real) proof. One clear hook, one clear ask.
2. **Record V1 on your phone.** Clean background, just talk to camera. Done beats perfect.
3. **Edit and caption.** Cut dead air in DaVinci Resolve (or any editor), splice in real proof screenshots, then auto-caption (CapCut or Descript).
4. **Host and embed.** Upload to a host (Vidalytics, Wistia, Vimeo…), copy the embed code, and place the VSL on your landing page.
5. **Create your pages.** Make a Facebook business page — personal name or company, per your niche — plus a matching Instagram page. Add photo, cover art, a post or two. **Do not** attach an ad account immediately.
6. **Launch the warm-up.** Build an **engagement** campaign, optimize for **page likes**, ~$5/day per ad set, targeting your contractor-age band (~29–64) in your real market geo. One simple, relevant creative.
7. **Stretch goals.** Record the shorter, more personal **pre-call VSL** as a slideshow with you cropped in a corner. A/B two warm-up creatives. Write your VSL's on-screen proof list as verifiable references.

### How you will know you are done

- ✅ Your VSL is **hosted and embeddable**, and actually appears on your landing page.
- ✅ Your Facebook **and** Instagram pages are **live**, named appropriately for your niche.
- ✅ Your **warm-up campaign is running** at ~$5/day, optimized for page likes, targeting your real audience (no bought-geo likes).
- ✅ Every claim in your VSL is truthful and something you could stand behind on a call.

> 💡 **Keep yourself honest:** if a line in your VSL would embarrass you when a prospect actually calls your reference, cut it. The whole "verify me" angle dies the moment one number doesn't check out.

## Practice exercises (optional extra reps)

> **What these are:** small, self-contained tasks, each giving focused practice on one idea. Optional and independent; the Capstone already touches all of them.

### Exercise 1: Hook rewrite (foundational)
Write three different opening hooks for your homepage VSL, each naming a specific pain your niche feels ("thrown money at Home Advisor and got burned…"). Pick the sharpest.

### Exercise 2: Homepage vs pre-call (intermediate)
Outline the same VSL twice — once for cold homepage traffic, once as the warmer, more personal, more proof-heavy pre-call version. List what you add or cut for each.

### Exercise 3: Warm-up plan on paper (advanced)
Design a compliant warm-up for a niche that is *not* home improvement (say, dental). Decide personal vs company page, the age/geo targeting, and write two sentences on why you'd refuse to buy cheap far-geo likes even though they're cheaper.
