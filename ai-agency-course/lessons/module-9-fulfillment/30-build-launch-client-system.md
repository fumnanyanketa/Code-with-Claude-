## A few plain-language basics first

This lesson uses some everyday terms. Here they are in plain words:

- **Pixel:** a small piece of tracking code that tells Facebook/Meta who took action (filled a form, booked). A "seasoned" or "warm" pixel has enough history that Meta already knows who to show ads to. A cold pixel is starting from zero.
- **Sub-account:** inside your CRM, a separate walled-off workspace for one client — their calendar, their phone number, their leads.
- **CRM (Customer Relationship Management):** the software that holds contacts, calendars, automations, and messaging. This course uses GoHighLevel (GHL).
- **Snapshot:** a saved template of an entire sub-account (automations, funnels, calendars) you can load into a fresh sub-account in one click.
- **ATP / A2P verification:** the phone-carrier approval that lets your CRM legally send SMS from a business number. Without it, texts silently fail.
- **Price anchor:** the number you put in an ad (e.g. "decks starting at $9,500") that sets the prospect's expectation before they ever call.
- **Funnel:** the landing page + form + booking calendar a lead flows through after clicking your ad.

You do not need to memorise these. Each is explained again the first time it matters.

## Why this lesson matters

Most beginners freeze at fulfillment because they have never seen it done live and assume it is as unpredictable as selling. It is not. *"Fulfillment is very repeatable. It's way more repeatable than the sale,"* — Owen. The sale changes with every prospect; fulfillment is nearly the same build every time. When you deliver *natively* — on your own page and pixel — you can get *"a client fully live in under 30 minutes"* and hand them a booked estimate the morning they pay. That speed is what kills buyer's remorse and buys you months of retention.

## Part 1: Native service delivery — one page, one pixel

There are two ways to run a client's ads. Owen calls one slow and one native.

**The slow way:** log into the client's Facebook, get added to their ad account as a partner, build their page, install a fresh pixel, warm it up, build their funnel. *"Onboarding just takes so long and your first impression will always look shaky… and a little scammy,"* — Owen. Worse: you spent time and money building *someone else's* business. If they churn, you keep nothing.

**The native way:** per niche, you run **one branded identity** — a general page like "Decking Solutions USA" or "Home Solutions USA" — with **one seasoned pixel**, and you duplicate one proven funnel per client. Same ad account, same pixel; the only per-client differences are a separate campaign and a separate funnel that points to that client's CRM sub-account.

| | Slow way (client's ad account) | Native way (your page + pixel) |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | Days of access requests | Instant — already set up |
| Pixel | Cold start every client | Seasoned; Meta already knows the buyer |
| First win | Slow | Can hand an estimate the day they pay |
| If they churn | You keep nothing | You keep the page, pixel, and funnel |
| Ad spend | On their card | On your business card (points/cash back) |

> 🔑 **Native delivery turns a custom project into a repeatable build: you perfect ONE system, then copy-paste it for every client.**

> ⚠️ **Reality check — be transparent.** Running a client's ads under a generic page like "Home Solutions USA" is efficient, but the client's leads are being generated on a page and pixel that are not theirs and that you keep if they leave. Do not let a client believe they own that asset or that the ads run on their own branded page when they don't. Tell them plainly how delivery works and what happens if the relationship ends. Efficiency is fine; misrepresentation is not — and it wrecks trust the moment they find out.

> ❌ **Don't imply generic AI job photos are the client's real work.** You will make AI creative (Lesson 16). A slick AI-generated "finished deck" is a stylized ad asset, not a photo of a job this contractor completed. Presenting invented images as the client's genuine past work is a false endorsement claim under FTC rules and can mislead the homeowner who calls. Label stock/AI creative as illustrative, and use the client's real job photos and reviews for proof.

## Part 2: Build the client's sub-account

One sub-account per client. That keeps automations client-specific and lets the client's team contact leads through the platform so you can actually track speed-to-lead.

**1. Start with a pre-ATP-verified sub-account.** *"It's very important that you start off with this so you don't have to wait… There should never be a delay because of [ATP verification],"* — Owen. Verify through your SMS provider ahead of time and buy a batch of numbers so a new sub-account is ready to text on day one.

> ⚠️ **Compliance is not optional.** ATP/A2P registration exists because business SMS is regulated (US TCPA + carrier 10DLC rules; stricter abroad under UK PECR, Canada CASL). Register your sender properly, only text leads who opted in via the client's ad/form, and honor opt-outs. "It got verified" is a technical step, not legal cover — get consent and opt-out right.

**2. Load the snapshot.** Import the client snapshot once to your CRM, then at the agency level open the sub-account → **Actions → Load snapshot → select all → import**. Wait a couple of minutes and you should see the automations, a funnel template, and two calendars (an estimate calendar and a reschedule calendar).

**3. Walk the automations at a high level — don't rebuild them.** The snapshot already contains the full lead lifecycle: new-lead alerts, first SMS, short/medium/long-term nurture (with *stop-on-response* so a replying lead exits the sequence), appointment-booked (which also fires the conversion event to Meta), reminders at 24h and 1h, a post-appointment "how did it go" email, plus cancel/no-show/reschedule paths and spam deletion. *"Keep in mind that all these don't even need to be touched… they're so evergreen,"* — Owen. They are written generically for home-improvement estimates, so they work across niches with light edits.

**4. Do the live buildout** — the part you actually touch per client:

- **Calendars & time zone.** Set the client's real availability (Owen's example: *"10 to 6 on weekdays… Saturdays 9 to 5, no bookings Sundays"*) and confirm the correct time zone. Match brand colors if you like.
- **Business profile.** Rename it so the client recognizes their account on login. Most of the rest doesn't matter for an in-person local business.
- **Phone number.** Buy a number in the client's local area code (their prospects trust a local number), set call **forwarding** to the client's office line, and turn on call recording so you can measure their speed-to-lead.
- **Custom fields & custom values.** Custom *fields* capture the survey answers ("Do you have a deck? How big? How old?"). Custom *values* are the fill-in-the-blanks that flow into every automation and page at once — agency email, client's lead-notification email/phone, Facebook pixel, Google review link, reschedule/estimate calendar links, company name, owner first name, office phone, website, confirmation URL. Fill these once and the whole account personalizes itself.

Finish with a quick test appointment to confirm messages fire correctly.

> 💡 **Alternatives to GoHighLevel.** GHL bundles CRM + funnels + calendar + automations + SMS. If you'd rather not lock in: HubSpot, Keap, Close, or Vendasta as all-in-ones; or assemble a cheaper, more flexible stack yourself — Cal.com/Calendly (booking) + Zapier or Make (automation) + Brevo/Mailchimp (email) + a form tool + Stripe. GHL saves you the wiring; a stack saves you money.

## Part 3: Steps 1–2 — customer profile and the price anchor

**Step 1 — Finalize the customer profile.** You cannot write an offer or set a price without understanding the business. On the onboarding call, collect: average job value, price range (the *floor* they'll accept), service area and radius, services they do and don't do, ideal customer (age, income tier, best-paying job type), and existing proof (real job photos, reviews). The **radius** matters because it tells you the total addressable market — Owen keeps it in a *"healthy range of 500k to 1.5 million people,"* which decides whether a friction-heavy landing page or a low-friction lead form fits (that logic lives in Module 5).

**Step 2 — Set the ad offer and price anchor.** For local home services, *"the number is the offer."* A price anchor is the figure in the ad that makes a prospect think "that's reasonable." Rules:

- **Anchor inside their job range, a bit above the floor.** Owen's decking client: floor $3.5K, average job $20K → he anchored at **$9,500**. High, because this client wants quality. Lower gets more leads but lower quality; higher gets fewer, better leads.
- **Never use round numbers.** *"5K, 8K, 10K… sounds like marketing."* Use odd specifics: 5.5K, 7.5K, 9.5K.
- **Wording tunes volume.** "Starting at $5.5K" pulls higher-quality leads; "for less than $5.5K" pulls more volume. Comma vs. no comma nudges the same lever.
- **60/40 rule.** Run ~60% of creatives toward volume, ~40% toward quality — enough volume to feed the pipeline, enough quality to protect the close rate.
- **Pre-handle the price objection in the ad.** If your anchor sits well under what the market expects, the prospect thinks "what's the catch?" Answer it: *"same materials as the big crews, we just don't pay for a fleet of trucks and a wall of billboards."*

> ✅ **What to do about it:** paste your customer profile into Claude (or ChatGPT/Gemini) and have it draft the offer and anchor from the job range — Owen does exactly this. Then sanity-check it against the floor yourself.

## Part 4: Steps 3–7 — creative, funnel, campaign, checklist, launch

These steps lean on earlier modules. Do not re-learn the mechanics here; assemble them for this client.

- **Step 3 — Creative & copy** (see **Lesson 16**). *"Creative is 80% of the result."* Build a few short AI video ads and image ads around the price offer. Research your niche's organic outliers on Facebook for a format that already works (Owen found sped-up job videos win in contracting). Log everything in a creative/copy/offer vault so you know what works.
- **Step 4 — Funnel & destination** (see **Lesson 12**). Build the funnel once, then **duplicate the project and just change the domain** for each new client — same pixel, new subdomain on the same root domain. Larger market → friction-heavy landing page; small market → low-friction lead form (Module 5 explains the friction logic).
- **Step 5 — Campaign buildout & naming** (see **Lesson 18** / Module 5). Build the campaign, wire the pixel and CRM integration, and use a simple naming convention so you can tell versions apart (bump to V1, V2 when you iterate).
- **Step 6 — Pre-launch checklist.** *"The most expensive mistakes come from rushing this process."* Before anything goes live, verify: price matches across ad/funnel, captions and CTA are correct, pixel fires, calendar and forwarding work, custom values are filled, a test lead flows end-to-end. Run it *every* launch.
- **Step 7 — Launch, then get a fast first win.** Your only week-one job is the client's first *shown* booked estimate. Speed kills buyer's remorse (which peaks ~24h after purchase).

> ⚠️ **Fronting ad spend is your cash-flow risk.** Native delivery lets you spend from your own card — and spending $100/day instead of $50/day roughly doubles results in the same time, which delights a nervous new client. But if you're covering the spend on a pay-per-result model, that money is *yours* until they pay. Protect it in the agreement (ad-spend reserves, or client's card on file for spend) so a churn or dispute doesn't leave you funding a stranger's marketing.

### The first seven days: don't touch it

The fastest way to kill a working campaign is to fiddle. *"Do not make decisions until an ad has spent at least three times the target cost per result."* If your target cost per lead is $40, wait until a single ad has spent ~$120 before judging it. Keep these in mind:

- **Leads come in clusters.** *"You can get zero leads for five days and then get four in one day."* Don't panic on fine metrics.
- **Regression to the mean.** Over enough spend, results stabilize.
- **Seasonality & external factors.** Sundays are slow; decking dies in winter; big-news days dip. Know your niche's seasons.
- **Audience pool exhaustion.** After a strong week or two, Meta needs time to find the next batch.
- If leads *fully* stop for 3–5 straight days and you've ruled everything else out, manually restart the campaign — after diagnosing, not before. (Kill/scale thresholds live in Module 5's "scaling with data.")

## Key takeaways

1. **One page, one pixel, duplicated funnels.** Native delivery = instant onboarding, a warm pixel, fast wins, and an asset you keep if the client churns.
2. **One sub-account per client, built live in under an hour.** Pre-verify ATP, load the snapshot, then only touch calendars, phone, and custom fields/values.
3. **The price anchor is the offer.** Inside the job range, a touch above the floor, never round, tuned with wording, 60/40 volume-to-quality.
4. **Assemble, don't re-learn.** Creative (L16), funnel (L12), campaign (L18/M5) plug into the same delivery frame.
5. **Checklist before launch, hands off after.** Wait 3× target cost per result before judging anything.

## Common pitfalls

- ❌ **Letting the client believe they own the page/pixel.** Be transparent about how their ads actually run and what you keep on churn.
- ❌ **Passing AI/stock creative off as the client's real completed jobs.** Label it illustrative; use their genuine photos and reviews for proof.
- ❌ **Fronting ad spend with no protection.** On a pay-per-result model, unprotected spend is your loss — put reserves or a card on file in the agreement.
- ❌ **Skipping ATP/A2P or texting non-opted-in leads.** SMS is regulated; register the sender and honor opt-outs.
- ❌ **Rushing the pre-launch checklist.** Price mismatches, dead pixels, and wrong CTAs are cheap to catch now, expensive later.
- ❌ **Tinkering in week one.** Most campaigns fail from over-adjusting, not bad ads. Wait for 3× the target cost per result.

## 🛠️ Capstone Project: full client-delivery dry run

> This is the main hands-on project for the lesson. You will feel what it's like to stand up a complete, launch-ready client system — without spending a dollar. You schedule; you don't have to run it.

### What you will build

A launch-ready delivery system for one imaginary (or real) local-service client in a niche you choose. Its pieces map straight to the lesson:

- A **CRM sub-account** with the snapshot loaded and the live buildout done (Part 2).
- A **customer profile + justified price anchor** (Part 3).
- A **funnel wired to the pixel and the sub-account** (Part 4).
- A **completed pre-launch checklist** (Part 4).

### Why this is the perfect practice

| Lesson idea | Where you use it in the dry run |
|---|---|
| Native delivery | You build on your own page/pixel, not the client's |
| Sub-account buildout | Calendars, phone, custom fields/values |
| Price anchor | You pick a number from the job range and defend it |
| Funnel + pixel wiring | You connect the duplicated funnel to this sub-account |
| Pre-launch checklist | You tick every box before "launch" |

### Milestones (build them in order, each one works on its own)

1. **Pick a niche and write a one-page customer profile.** Average job value, floor, radius, ideal customer, proof. Invent realistic numbers if you have no real client.
2. **Create and ATP-verify a sub-account, then load the snapshot.** Confirm you see the automations, funnel, and two calendars.
3. **Do the live buildout.** Set calendar availability and time zone, buy/assign a local-area number with forwarding, and fill in the custom values.
4. **Set the price anchor and write one sentence defending it.** Show it's inside the job range, above the floor, non-round, and note your 60/40 split.
5. **Duplicate the funnel, change the domain, and wire the pixel + sub-account.** Send one test lead through end to end.
6. **Run the pre-launch checklist.** Price match, captions/CTA, pixel fires, calendar + forwarding, custom values, test lead. Fix anything red.
7. **Stretch goals.** Draft the 60/40 creative brief; write the first-7-days messaging plan; set your 3× target-cost-per-result kill threshold in writing.

### How you will know you are done

- ✅ Sub-account is built and ATP-verified, with the snapshot loaded and the live buildout complete.
- ✅ Your price anchor is written down *with* a one-line justification tied to the job range.
- ✅ Every pre-launch checklist item is ticked, and a test lead flowed from funnel to CRM.

> 💡 **Keep yourself honest:** schedule, don't necessarily spend. The goal is a system that *could* launch cleanly today — not a live ad bill. If you can't defend your price anchor in one sentence, you're not done.

## Practice exercises (optional extra reps)

> **What these are:** small, self-contained tasks, each giving focused practice on one idea. Optional and independent; the Capstone already touches all of them.

### Exercise 1: Price-anchor reps (foundational)
Take three fake profiles — floor $2K/avg $8K, floor $4K/avg $15K, floor $6K/avg $30K — and write a non-round anchor for each with a one-line reason. Note which wording ("starting at" vs "for less than") you'd use.

### Exercise 2: Snapshot walk (intermediate)
Open the automations in your sub-account and trace one lead's full path: new-lead alert → first SMS → nurture → appointment booked. Write down the one custom value each step depends on.

### Exercise 3: Checklist under pressure (advanced)
Deliberately break three things in your dry run (mismatch the price, remove a custom value, disable the pixel). Then run the checklist and confirm it catches all three before "launch."
