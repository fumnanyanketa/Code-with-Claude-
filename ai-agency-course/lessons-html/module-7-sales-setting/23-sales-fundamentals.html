In one sentence

High-ticket selling is not a script you recite but an energy you transfer — you use genuine presence and truth to open a gap between where a prospect is and where they want to be, then position yourself as the bridge, and everything (tone, intention, self-image, state, identity) exists to make that transfer honest and believable.

First-principles companion

💡 The durable idea behind this lesson. The tactics are new, but the science is decades old. For the timeless, tool-agnostic version:

  • Mehrabian, Silent Messages (1971) — the origin of the "7% words / 38% tone / 55% body" figures Owen uses. Read the real study, not the meme version (see the reality check in Part 4 — it is one of the most over-generalized findings in all of communication).
  • NeuroSelling by Jeff Bloomfield — Owen's recommended book on why buying is an emotional, subconscious act your logic only rationalizes afterward. This is the first-principles account of "buy on emotion, justify with logic."

A few plain-language basics first

This lesson uses some everyday sales terms. Here they are in plain words:

  • High-ticket sale: turning an interested person into a paying client for something that costs a larger sum. Owen's rough line is roughly $2,000 and up (he'd say $1,000 doesn't really count), and it usually closes on one video call. Numbers are illustrative — adjust to your niche.
  • Prospect: a potential buyer who is on a call with you but has not yet paid.
  • Discovery: the questioning part of the call where you figure out where the prospect really is and where they really want to be.
  • The gap: the emotional distance between a prospect's current painful situation and the future they crave. Selling is mostly the act of making that gap visible.
  • Container word: a word or phrase a prospect uses that has extra emotion packed inside it ("work's been slow", "I needed help"). It's a door into a deeper conversation.
  • State: the condition you're in right now — mood, posture, thoughts, energy. On a call it's contagious.
  • Close rate: the share of sales calls that end in a paid sale. A "good" agency close rate is often talked about around 20–30%; treat that as a target, not a promise.

You do not need to memorise these. Each is explained again the first time it matters.

Why this lesson matters

You just spent Module 6 learning to book calls. A booked call is worthless if you freeze, read a robotic script, and stay on the surface. Owen is blunt about it: "sales isn't one of those things that you can just kind of watch a few videos at and be good at… it comes from you… but also repetition, because you can watch these videos all day… but never have the skills because you didn't put in the hours." This lesson gives you the mental model so your reps actually compound. The single most freeing idea in it: the exact words you say are only about 7% of what closes the deal. That means you can stop hunting for the perfect line and start being a present, honest human being — which, it turns out, sells far better.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  1. Explain why sales is a continuum, not a binary, and why no single script can guarantee a close rate.
  2. Use the gap/bridge model: run discovery that separates "where they are" from "where they want to be," then position yourself as the bridge.
  3. Name the four parts of communication (words, tonality, intention, self-image) and stop over-weighting words.
  4. Practice presence and truth — selling scriptless and challenging vague numbers for the real ones.
  5. Move a conversation through the three levels (surface → action → transformational/identity) using container words and "I am" as signposts.
  6. Run a simple state-management ritual (anchor words + breathing) so one bad call doesn't wreck your whole day.
  7. Adopt identity-based selling — focus on intention over current ability — while staying on the honest side of the line between persuasion and manipulation.

Prerequisites

  • Module 6 (cold SMS / calling) — you are now booking calls, so you need something to do on them.
  • A willingness to do reps. Owen's rule of thumb: it takes an average of about 100 conducted sales calls to reach $10k/month, and your first ~25 calls may all be no's. (These are his figures — your mileage varies with niche and experience.)

Part 1: Sales is repetition, and it is a continuum — not a binary

Before any technique, absorb the doctrine Owen repeats more than anything else: the more sales calls you take, the more money you make. He went back through his old recordings and found it took him exactly 50 calls to hit his first $10k month — "the first 25 were all no's." His whole point: "imagine if I quit on the 24th no… instead of pushing toward that 26th call where I got my first yes."

🔑 You do not have a strategy problem, you have a volume problem — until you've conducted 100 real calls, the answer to "why am I not at 10k?" is almost always "take more calls."

The second foundational idea: sales is a continuum, not a binary. There is no one-size-fits-all script because no two prospects are the same — "different energy, different lives, different family, different expectations, different trauma, different levels of buy-in." Some calls are light and full of jokes and the person just buys; others go so deep the prospect "literally start[s] crying and rethinking their entire identity." Most sit between. A good rep can close one type of person; a great rep keeps a consistent close rate across all of them — and that only comes from reps.

Owen's philosophy for the whole module, worth writing down: "sell scriptless, leverage neuroscience, use presence, and utilize truth in order to drive the transformation."

The misconception list

Owen clears the deck with a rapid-fire list of things beginners wrongly believe. Here it is as a table:

The myth The reality (per Owen)
You must be high-energy like a car salesman False. Stay calm and in the middle so your tone can fluctuate up or down. Start high and you can only go down — and drag the prospect down with you.
The best salespeople are extroverts False. Introverts often excel — they listen more and speak with intention.
Follow-up is what closes deals False. "Follow-up is for people that can't close."
Great salespeople are born False. They've simply sold more and taken more calls.
You must sound professional and polished False. You have to sound genuine, like a real person.
A script makes my close rate predictable False. Everyone is different; one script can't guarantee a rate.
I must close as many clients as humanly possible False. "Only close the people that you can actually help."
Business owners hate being on sales calls False. They'll pay thousands to a good salesperson who fixes their problem.
Nobody will pay 2–5K on one 45-minute call False. "If they believe in the transformation, they will justify the cost. Price only becomes an issue when your belief is weak."

💡 Notice the honest one hiding in the list. "Only close the people you can actually help." That single line is the ethical spine of this entire lesson — hold onto it, because the machinery you're about to learn is powerful enough to talk people into things, and this is the rule that keeps it clean.

Part 2: The neuroscience — people buy on emotion, then justify with logic

Owen's framing: "people buy with the limbic system — the emotional brain — first, and then afterwards they justify their decision with logic." His rough split: 80–90% emotion, 10–20% logic. The classic beginner mistake is trying to logic someone into a decision that was never made logically in the first place.

Two consequences fall straight out of this:

  1. You need emotional momentum — the compounding emotional weight that builds during a call. You cannot manufacture it with facts.
  2. This is why high-ticket closes on one call. Momentum decays. Owen: "as soon as they hop off the sales call with you, there's a plant that gets seated in their brain… and it's doubt." Two or three calls means days of that doubt growing between conversations. One call preserves the momentum.

🔑 Buying is ~80–90% emotional and mostly subconscious; logic is the story people tell themselves afterward. Sell to the emotion, then hand them the logic to justify it.

⚠️ Reality check — this is where technique shades into pressure. "Close on one call before doubt grows" is genuinely good for momentum and is exactly the mechanism a high-pressure closer abuses to stop someone from thinking it over or sleeping on it. The tell is your intention (Part 4): compressing the timeline to protect a decision the person genuinely wants is service; compressing it to prevent them from making a considered choice is manipulation. If a prospect needs to check the budget with a partner or actually can't afford it, "never let them off the call" becomes a way to extract money from someone you may not be able to help. Pressure-selling wins the transaction and loses the retention — refunds, chargebacks, bad word-of-mouth in a small local market. Aim to make the decision feel easy because it's genuinely right, not because you gave them no exit.

Part 3: Why people buy — purpose, agitation, awareness, and the gap

People do not buy because of pain. They buy because of purpose — the purpose of escaping pain and moving toward an identity they emotionally crave. Owen's own example: he paid ~$5k for a coaching program "not because I was in insane amounts of pain… [but] because I wanted to move towards the identity of being a more successful entrepreneur and also escape the pain of not being as successful as I know I could be."

Here's the mechanism. Before a prospect ever talks to you, life has already done half your work:

   AWARENESS  ──►  they've seen (via social media, peers, competitors)
                   that a better life/business is possible
        +
   AGITATION  ──►  they live in a current situation that now feels
                   not-good-enough compared to that possibility

They arrive aware and agitated but coping. Your job on the call is to make the gap between those two undeniable:

   [ WHERE THEY ARE ]  ← you define this precisely via discovery
            │
            │   ← THE GAP  (painful, they want to escape it)
            │
   [ WHERE THEY WANT TO BE ]  ← you define this too

   ══════════  YOU / YOUR OFFER = THE BRIDGE  ══════════

You ask a ton of questions, dig deep, and in doing so you pull "where they are" and "where they want to be" apart until the distance is visible and uncomfortable. "This gap is everything," Owen says. "The key is that you have to position yourself as the bridge between the gap." Only when they truly believe you are that bridge will they buy.

🔑 Discovery isn't information-gathering — it's gap-building. Every good question widens the felt distance between now and the wanted future, and then you show them you're the way across.

Part 4: The four parts of communication (words are only 7%)

Owen asks you to pause and guess which of four things matters most on a call. Most people guess "the words." They're very wrong. The four parts:

# Part What it is Rough weight (Owen's model)
1 Words The literal thing you say 7%
2 Tonality How you say it 38%
3 Intention What you actually want for them ~27.5%
4 Self-image Who you believe you are ~27.5%

The 7% / 38% / 55% figures come from Mehrabian's rule. Owen splits the "55% body language" half of Mehrabian's model into intention and self-image (his own interpretation — the two 27.5% figures are his, not Mehrabian's).

⚠️ Reality check — Mehrabian's 7/38/55 is real research but is one of the most misapplied stats around. Mehrabian's experiments were narrow: they measured how people judge feelings and attitudes when the words and the tone/face contradict each other (e.g. saying "I'm fine" in a cold voice). He never claimed that only 7% of all communication is words. Mehrabian himself warned against the generalization. So the honest takeaway is not "words don't matter" — it's "when your words and your delivery disagree, people believe your delivery." On a sales call that's still a powerful lesson: your tone, intention, and self-belief have to match your message, or the prospect trusts the mismatch over the script. Use the 7% as a liberation from perfectionism, not as a fact about the universe.

Words (7%). A decent phrase with great energy beats a perfect line delivered with hesitation, every time. Stop hunting for the perfect rebuttal.

Tonality (38%). A downward inflection signals certainty ("Here's what we're going to cover…"); an upward one signals curiosity ("So how's that going for you?"); neutral is safe and reliable. His illustration: "Mom, go take out the trash" vs "Hey Mom, can you take out the trash?" — same words, opposite result. Over-exaggerate your tone until it becomes habit, and use silence to create gravity. You'll mostly notice tone problems only when you watch the call back.

Intention (~27.5%). "The invisible force behind your words. People sense it." If your intention is just "get a name on the calendar" or "get their cash," they subconsciously feel it — and they no-show, or they don't buy. Owen's pre-call move: ask "Why am I really doing this?" and convince yourself you want the outcome for them more than the sale for yourself. Make the mental shift from "How could I close this prospect?" to "What's the truth they need to see right now?"

Self-image (~27.5%, but he calls it the real 99%). "People don't buy from who you think you are. They buy from how you carry who you are — and how you carry who you are comes from who you think you are." If you see yourself as a nervous beginner, that leaks through posture, pauses, and breath, and they won't trust you with their money.

💡 Nuance on "brainwash yourself" and "see yourself as the best in the world." Genuine confidence sells and is worth building. But there's a healthy version and a delusional version. Believing "I can deliver a great result and I'll do everything to make sure they succeed" is earned confidence. Chanting "I'm the best marketer in the world" when you've never run an ad is a story that will collide with reality the moment you have to fulfill. Build the self-image and build the skill underneath it, so the belief is true.

Part 5: Presence and truth — sell scriptless, and challenge for the real numbers

The market is drowning in "overused scripts, AI robotic pitches… no authenticity." That's your opening. Presence means giving the conversation your full attention — "no multitasking, no glancing at frameworks, no reading scripts, no searching things up… focus both eyes and your whole mind on the prospect." When you're truly present, "the right questions will come to you" because you're actually hearing the person instead of rehearsing your next line.

Truth is the other half. Prospects bend the numbers — "maybe just a bend the truth a little bit." If they say they get "six to eight leads a month," that's not a real number. Owen's move:

"Was it six or was it eight? Last month, how many exactly?" … "You're doing one to two jobs a month — how many did you get done so far this month?" "Zero." "What about last month?" "One." …

By pinning down exact numbers, you "cause a ton of indirect pain and separate the distance" — i.e., you build the gap. Challenge vague answers; don't let BS slide. Someone who lies their way through the call will lie their way off it.

A tale of two calls. Owen contrasts two reps selling to a roofing contractor:

The script reader The present operator
Stares at a list of questions, eyes on the screen Opens naturally — "Is that a Detroit Lions flag behind you?" — eyes on the prospect
"What's the biggest problem?" → "Lead flow." → next question → next question Hears a real detail (a divorce caused by overwork) and stops: "Wait — you got divorced because of work? How did that happen?"
Collects data, stays on the surface, prospect feels interviewed Digs into what actually hurts, prospect feels heard
Doesn't close Closes

The difference isn't the words. It's that one is present and the other is performing.

✅ What to do about it: run your next call with no script visible. Keep one sticky note with three questions you tend to forget, and otherwise just listen and follow the container words. If you must take notes, jot only the container words (see Part 6) so you can quote them back later.

Part 6: The three levels of conversation (the glacier)

Picture a glacier. Most of it is underwater. To close, you must get to level two or three — nobody buys from the surface.

  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ waterline ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  LEVEL 1 · SURFACE     "I want more leads."
   └ data collection · binary answers · no emotion · you sound like everyone
  ─────────────────────────────────────────
  LEVEL 2 · ACTION      "I want more work so I don't have to worry about money."
   └ emotional awareness · they take responsibility · SIGNAL: container words
  ─────────────────────────────────────────
  LEVEL 3 · TRANSFORMATION / IDENTITY
   "…so I can provide for my kids and be a role model."
   └ identity awareness · they see a truer future self · SIGNAL: "I am…"

Level 1 — surface awareness. "How many leads did you get last month? What's your biggest constraint?" You're collecting data. Necessary, but if you stay here they "walk away feeling like nothing even happened." People stuck in their own heads ("what do I ask next?") get trapped at level 1.

Level 2 — emotional awareness (the action level). Transition here by asking why and how is that affecting you? "What have you tried? How long has this been happening?" Your goal: hold up a mirror so they feel the emotional weight of their situation and take responsibility for it. The signal you've arrived: container words — words packed with meaning ("work's been slow," "I needed help," "burning desire"). Write these down; they're doors. You can quote them back later: "I thought you said work's been slow?" Most decent reps live here — and that's enough for a solid close rate.

A tool for level 2: pre-framing. Don't machine-gun questions — that feels like an interrogation and people close up. Pre-framing is a one-sentence bridge that sets up the next question so it lands soft:

❌ "How is all this affecting how you show up to the job site?" (feels salesy, abrupt) ✅ "So we've had a chance to look at how things are going… I just want to understand a bit more — is all this actually affecting how you show up to the job site, or not?"

Level 3 — identity awareness (transformation). The deepest part. Transition from level 2 with "How would that change how you see yourself?" or by asking how others (family, employees, peers) see them. The move is expansion — you take what they've said about what they do and expand it into who they are. You "create a mirror with their future self in it," so they see who they'd have to become to get the result. The signal you've arrived: the prospect starts saying "I am" ("I'm not the type of person who…"). Owen tells the story of his own $5k purchase — the rep "let me see who I could become… and with his energy, allowed me to step into that version of myself."

You can gently cast an identity: "I noticed you're more into pushing yourself, more growth-minded — have you always been like that?" People agree, then stay consistent with the identity they just claimed (consistency is a powerful human drive).

⚠️ Reality check — this is the sharpest edge in the lesson. "Challenge their identity to the breaking point," "make them cry and question their whole identity," and casting identities they then feel compelled to live up to are potent psychological levers. On the honest side, you're helping a genuinely-fitting buyer see a truth about themselves they already sensed. On the dark side, you're destabilizing someone's self-concept to extract a payment they may regret. Two guardrails: (1) only run this on people your offer can genuinely help — the misconception list already told you not to close everyone; (2) if getting to a "yes" requires breaking someone rather than clarifying something for them, that's your signal to stop, not push. The identity you help them step into has to be one you can actually help them fulfill.

Objections are pre-handled here. Owen's stance: "you shouldn't have that many objections if you're good enough… objection-handling issues are usually just discovery issues." If in discovery you already surfaced that the spouse would obviously be on board (because they'd get more time together), then "I need to ask my wife" barely survives at the end. Deeper discovery beats slick rebuttals.

🔑 Before you pitch, present a price, or share your screen — get to level three. Container words tell you you're at level two; "I am" tells you you're at level three. If you're still getting one-word surface answers, they are not ready to buy.

Part 7: State management — your mood is contagious

Your state is the condition you're in: mood, body, posture, thoughts, emotions. It matters because buying is emotional and emotions transfer. Owen invokes mirror neurons: "people will quite literally reflect how you feel on the call." Come in confident and upbeat and their brain mirrors it; come in anxious and sad and they catch that instead — and don't buy.

The enemy is your built-in negativity bias — Owen's rule of thumb is that for every positive thought your brain throws up ~three negatives, a survival feature that floods you with worst-case scenarios, cortisol, and shaky-handed fight-or-flight right when you need to be calm. You beat it with an emotional baseline — the ability to not get knocked off-center by things that don't actually matter (the driver who flips you off; the fifth cold-call prospect who says "never call me again"). Owen's blunt test: that person is a stranger on the other side of the planet you'll never speak to again — why hand them your state?

The anchor-words ritual (do this now):

  1. On paper, draw a line down the middle. Left column: reasons you lose sales/bookings (or the traits of people who do). Right column: words/qualities that remind you of your best, in-flow, successful state.
  2. Get to 20–30 words on the right. Then find the three words that make you feel something when you read them. (Owen's example set: grounded, curious, challenging.)
  3. Print those three words and tape them above your monitor, or set them as your wallpaper.
  4. Before and after every call: take three deep breaths, then say your three anchor words and mean them. After a bad call, the same ritual stops the damage bleeding into the next one.

Over enough reps the words "seep into your identity" — first habit, then instinct, then just who you are. Supporting habits: in the morning tell yourself "today's going to be a good day" and list what you're grateful for; watch one of your good calls in the morning to set your state, and watch a bad call at night to learn (never before calls — it'll tank you).

🔑 A consistent close rate comes from a consistent state, not a consistent script. One bad call should cost you one call, not the whole day.

Part 8: Identity-based selling — intention over ability

Owen's anchor line: "Who you are off the call is who you are on the call." Your identity leaks into everything, so you build the salesperson off the call — through repeated action over ~30 days (habit), 60–90 days (instinct), until it seeps into identity. Every thought that doesn't serve you ("I should've closed that, I'm bad at sales"), you kill — refuse to entertain it.

The key reframe, and maybe the most useful single idea in the lesson:

"Never focus on your ability. Ability traps you — intention is infinite."

If you focus on current ability, the story is "I've never closed a deal, so it's not my ability to close" — a ceiling. If you focus on intention, the story is "I intend to become great, and I'll take the action to grow" — no ceiling. "A good salesman sells a product. A great salesman sells a future… people don't buy products, they buy feelings, status, identity, and results."

💡 Keep it honest. "Intention is infinite" is a fantastic motivational frame and it can become an excuse to sell past your real ability — promising results you can't yet deliver. Reconcile the two: let intention drive how hard you work and learn, but let honest ability set what you promise. Sell the transformation you can actually facilitate.

Key takeaways

  1. Reps beat everything. ~100 conducted calls to $10k/month; your first 25 may be no's. The question is never "why am I not at 10k" — it's "how many calls have I taken?"
  2. Sales is a continuum, not a binary. No script guarantees a close rate because no two people are the same. Sell scriptless.
  3. People buy on emotion (~80–90%) and justify with logic. One call preserves emotional momentum before doubt grows.
  4. Purpose, not pain. They buy to escape pain toward a craved identity. Discovery builds the gap; you are the bridge.
  5. Words are ~7%. Tonality, intention, and self-image carry the rest — but the real Mehrabian lesson is "when delivery and words disagree, people believe delivery."
  6. Presence + truth. Be fully present; challenge vague numbers for the exact ones — that's how you build the gap honestly.
  7. Three levels: surface (data) → action (container words) → identity ("I am"). Reach level 2 or 3 before you pitch.
  8. Manage your state with anchor words + breathing; a bad call costs one call, not the day.
  9. Intention over ability. Ability traps you; intention is infinite. Build the identity off the call.

Common pitfalls

  • ❌ Reading a script and staying on the surface. You'll collect data, feel like you asked "good questions," and never move the gap. Put the script away.
  • ❌ Trying to logic someone into a yes. The decision is emotional; facts alone don't move it. Build emotional momentum first, then hand them the logic.
  • ❌ Over-focusing on the perfect line. It's 7%. A decent phrase with real confidence beats a perfect line with hesitation.
  • ❌ Quoting Mehrabian as "words don't matter." They do — the finding is about contradiction between words and delivery. Don't repeat the meme as fact.
  • ❌ Closing everyone you can. Only close people you can genuinely help. High-pressure closing of poor-fit buyers wrecks retention, invites chargebacks, and burns your reputation in a small local market.
  • ❌ Being a victim to your state. One flipped-off-in-traffic moment leaking into five calls is a self-inflicted wound. Run the ritual.
  • ❌ Focusing on current ability. "I've never closed" becomes a ceiling. Switch to intention.

🛠️ Capstone Project: Your scriptless "Sales Operating Principles" one-pager (+ 5 mock calls)

This is the main hands-on project for the lesson. Owen's own action items are to complete five mock calls and read NeuroSelling. We wrap both around a one-page operating doc so your reps have a spine — without turning it into a script.

What you will build

A single page you can glance at before a call (not during), plus five completed mock calls. The page has three sections, each mapped to a lesson idea:

  • Your gap/bridge for your specific niche (Part 3) — the "where they are," the "where they want to be," and the one sentence that makes you the bridge.
  • Your state ritual (Part 7) — your 3 anchor words and your exact pre-call routine.
  • Your three levels (Part 6) — 2–3 example questions each for surface, action, and identity, plus the container words you expect to hear.

Why this is the perfect practice

Lesson idea Where you use it in the one-pager / mock calls
Gap/bridge (Part 3) Section 1: write your niche's gap and your bridge sentence
Presence & truth (Part 5) Mock calls: no script visible; challenge every vague number
Three levels (Part 6) Section 3: example questions per level; practice reaching level 2–3 in the mocks
Container words / "I am" (Part 6) Section 3: list the container words you expect; catch them live
State management (Part 7) Section 2: 3 anchor words + breathing ritual, run before each mock
Intention over ability (Part 8) Do the mocks even though you're "not ready" — that's the point

Milestones (build them in order, each one works on its own)

  1. Draft your gap/bridge. For your niche (e.g. roofers), write one line for the agitated current state, one for the craved future, and one bridge sentence ("My ads + follow-up take you from 1–2 jobs a month to a booked calendar"). Illustrative — make it true for your offer.
  2. Run the anchor-words exercise. Two columns, 20–30 words, pick your 3. Write your exact pre/post-call ritual (3 breaths + say the words). Print it or set it as wallpaper.
  3. Write your three-level question bank. 2–3 example questions for each level, plus a "why?"/"how's that affecting you?" transition to level 2 and a "how would that change how you see yourself?" transition to level 3. List the container words you expect in your niche.
  4. Assemble the one-pager. Sections 1–3 on a single page. If it needs a second page, it's too long — cut.
  5. Book five mock calls. Ideally five different people who are at your level or better (Owen: don't only practice with fellow beginners). A "mock call" is a practice sales call with someone in your circle or program role-playing a prospect.
  6. Run the five mocks scriptless. Page out of sight. Goal each time: reach level 2 (container words) and attempt level 3 ("I am"). Run your state ritual before each.
  7. Stretch goals. Record the mocks (with consent). Watch one back and mark where you stayed on the surface, where you missed a container word, and where your tone didn't match your words. Buy and start NeuroSelling by Jeff Bloomfield, taking notes as if it were a lesson.

How you will know you are done

  • ✅ Your one-pager fits on a single page and you could hand it to a friend and have them understand your selling approach.
  • ✅ You completed five mock calls with the page not visible during the call.
  • ✅ In at least one mock you can point to the moment you heard a container word and the moment you heard (or steered toward) an "I am" statement.
  • ✅ You ran your anchor-words + breathing ritual before every mock and can say your 3 words from memory.
  • ✅ You can name one specific thing to fix from a call you watched back.

💡 Keep yourself honest: the temptation is to turn the one-pager into a script and read it on calls. Don't. It's a pre-flight doc — you read it before takeoff, then fly by listening. If you catch yourself glancing at it mid-call, you've lost presence.

Practice exercises (optional extra reps)

What these are: small, self-contained tasks, each giving focused practice on one idea. Optional and independent; the Capstone already touches all of them, so feel free to skip straight to it.

Exercise 1: Container-word hunt (foundational)

Watch any recorded sales call (yours, a peer's, or a public one) with the sound on and a notepad. Write down every container word you hear — the phrases with extra emotional weight. Aim for 10. This trains the ear you'll use live.

Exercise 2: The truth challenge (intermediate)

Take one vague answer you commonly get ("six to eight leads") and script three escalating truth-challenge follow-ups that pin it to an exact number ("Was it six or eight? Last month exactly? The month before?"). Practice saying them out loud with a calm, curious tone — then throw the script away and do it live.

Exercise 3: Level 2 → Level 3 transition (advanced)

Role-play (or self-record) a single transition: take a container word, expand it to an identity statement using "How would that change how you see yourself?" and keep going until your imaginary prospect would plausibly say "I am / I'm not the type of person who…". Do it three times with three different container words.

Cheat sheet

SALES FUNDAMENTALS — SCRIPTLESS SELLING

DOCTRINE
  More calls = more money. ~100 calls to $10k/mo; first ~25 may be no's.
  Sales is a CONTINUUM, not binary. No script guarantees a close rate.
  Philosophy: sell scriptless · leverage neuroscience · presence · truth.

NEUROSCIENCE
  Buy ~80-90% EMOTION, justify with logic. Don't logic them into a yes.
  Emotional momentum decays -> ONE-CALL close (doubt grows between calls).
  ⚠ one-call momentum is honest; "never let them off the call" is not.

WHY PEOPLE BUY = PURPOSE (escape pain -> craved identity)
  Awareness + Agitation --> discovery opens THE GAP --> you = the BRIDGE.

FOUR PARTS OF COMMUNICATION (Mehrabian-derived)
  Words 7% · Tonality 38% · Intention ~27.5% · Self-image ~27.5%
  Real lesson: when delivery & words DISAGREE, people believe delivery.
  Decent phrase + great energy > perfect line + hesitation.
  Intention: shift "how do I close this?" -> "what truth must they see?"

PRESENCE & TRUTH
  Full attention. No script/multitask. Right questions come from listening.
  Challenge vague numbers for exact ones -> builds the gap. Don't let BS slide.

THREE LEVELS (glacier)
  1 SURFACE  data, one-word answers            (nobody buys here)
  2 ACTION   emotional awareness  SIGNAL: container words   (why? how's it affecting you?)
  3 IDENTITY future self          SIGNAL: "I am"            (how would that change how you see yourself?)
  Reach level 2-3 BEFORE you pitch. Objections = weak discovery.

STATE MANAGEMENT (mirror neurons: your mood is contagious)
  3 anchor words + 3 breaths before/after every call.
  Watch a GOOD call AM, a BAD call PM. Bad call = 1 call lost, not the day.

IDENTITY-BASED SELLING
  Who you are off the call = who you are on the call.
  Ability TRAPS you. Intention is INFINITE. Kill unserving thoughts.

ETHICS GUARDRAIL
  Only close people you can genuinely help. Honesty > pressure.
  Sell the transformation you can actually deliver.
