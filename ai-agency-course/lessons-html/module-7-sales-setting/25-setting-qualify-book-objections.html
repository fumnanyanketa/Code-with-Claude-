In one sentence

First-principles companion

💡 The durable idea behind this lesson. The tools change; the psychology of a qualifying conversation does not. For the timeless version:

  • SPIN Selling by Neil Rackham (book). The research behind "let the numbers reveal the pain" — questions that surface a problem convert better than pitching a solution. Owen's "build a little pain via truthful numbers" is SPIN's Problem and Implication questions in miniature.

A few plain-language basics first

This lesson uses some everyday terms. Here they are in plain words:

  • Setting call: a short first call whose only job is to book a longer sales ("closing") call — not to sell. You qualify, build interest, and lock in a time.
  • Qualifying: checking whether a prospect is actually a good fit (enough money, time, and capacity) before you spend a closing call on them.
  • Reverse qualification: Owen's term for gently acting as if the prospect might not be a good fit, so they start selling you on why they are. He also calls it "tactical skepticism."
  • Tie-down: getting a small, explicit commitment ("press yes to confirm") so the booking sticks.
  • Pickup rate (PUR): of the people you dial, the share who answer.
  • Booking rate / ABR (Appointment Booking Rate): of the people you talk to (or dial), the share who book a call.
  • Show rate: of the people who booked, the share who actually show up.
  • Speed to lead: how fast you contact someone after they raise their hand. Faster is better.
  • CRM: the software that holds your contacts, notes, calendar, and text automations (Owen uses GoHighLevel; alternatives below).

You do not need to memorise these. Each is explained again the first time it matters.

Why this lesson matters

In Module 6 you learned how to get a business owner on the phone. That is worthless if the call goes nowhere. This lesson is where a dial becomes a confirmed appointment that actually shows up. Owen is blunt about the single biggest leak: "The biggest reason why people don't show up to calls is because the setter hangs up the call before they actually get an email or get anything of substance." — Owen. Master the tie-down and the three metrics, and you stop wasting the outreach you worked so hard for.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  1. Build a small, honest amount of pain on a setting call using truthful numbers, not emotional depth.
  2. Decide when to ask "why now?" (and when it makes no sense).
  3. Qualify a prospect against income / time / capacity criteria for your niche, and reverse-qualify them ethically.
  4. Tie down a booking live on the phone so it holds.
  5. Handle the common setting objections with a repeatable acknowledge → flip → reframe pattern.
  6. Measure and improve your pickup rate, booking rate (ABR), and show rate — inside the compliance rules from Lesson 22.

Prerequisites

Part 1: Build a little pain — with truthful numbers, not therapy

Pain drives sales. But on a setting call, you want only a little of it. "On setting calls, we don't want too much pain. You do want to build some." — Owen. Business owners are what Owen calls PNA-energy types — practical, action-based, and harder to move emotionally. Driving into "you never see your kids at night" will get you hung up on.

The move is to enter level two (the action level) just far enough to make the numbers honest. Ask how many jobs or leads they do, and gently make the real number apparent:

Owen's example: they say "10 to 20 leads a month." You dig — last month was 10, before that 8, before that 6. There was never a 20. "So usually it's more like four to ten. Okay, gotcha." — Owen. The truth is the pain. You never exaggerated; you just made reality clear.

🔑 On a setting call, the truthful number is enough pain. Don't go deep — go clear.

⚠️ Reality check. "Making the numbers apparent" only works if you stay honest. The moment you inflate their problem or your results to manufacture pain, you cross into misleading claims (FTC territory) — and business owners smell it instantly, which kills the call anyway.

Part 2: "Why now?" — powerful, but only for warm leads

Finding out why they're on the call right now builds urgency and commitment. But the timing of the question has to make sense:

  • ✅ Do ask for inbound and paid-ad leads: "You've probably been seeing these ads for months — why now? What's changed?"
  • ❌ Never ask on any cold outreach — cold call, cold SMS, cold DM. You reached out to them; they made no conscious decision to come to you, so "why now?" is nonsense and exposes you.

✅ What to do about it: tag each lead's source in your CRM. If the source is "cold," the "why now?" question is off the table.

Part 3: Qualify — and reverse-qualify

Qualification decides who you book and who you don't. It's niche-specific, but the rule of thumb is income, time, and job capacity. Owen's roofing criteria (illustrative — adapt to your niche):

Criterion Owen's roofing cut-off (example) Why
Income Won't take clients under ~$500k/yr Below that they can't afford results or absorb the volume
Time Skips owners retiring/selling/checked-out this year It's not their priority; churn is guaranteed
Capacity Needs enough subcontractors + sales reps to handle the jobs If they can't service the leads you send, you both fail

"Don't just sign and close everyone that comes to you. Otherwise you'll have really bad churn." — Owen. Write your criteria into an SOP and keep them in mind on every call.

Reverse qualification is Owen's persuasion twist — "tactical skepticism." You act slightly unsure they qualify, so they start convincing you:

"I mean, are you sure you'd even have the time for this? Do you even want to grow the business? Because we only work with growth-minded people, and I don't know if we can even help you." — Owen (illustrative).

Done with a bad tone, this gets you hung up on. Done warm, "people will actually try to convince you why they're qualified." It works because these owners are used to being in authority; breaking that pattern makes you feel different.

⚠️ Reality check — this is a persuasion frame, keep it honest. Reverse qualification and lines like "we don't take just anyone with a pulse and a credit card" only stay ethical if they're true: you genuinely have real qualification criteria and you'd genuinely turn away a bad-fit client. If you'd actually take anyone who pays, then pretending otherwise is manipulation — and manipulated clients churn, dispute charges, and trash your local reputation. Use the frame only because it reflects a real standard you hold.

Part 4: Take notes in the CRM

Every setting call gets notes: income, goals, current situation, energy type, background. Whether you close it yourself or hand it to a closer, "it's crucial you take notes." — Owen. Put them straight in the contact record so they're waiting for you (or the closer) on the closing call. That gets the surface-level data collection out of the way, freeing the closing call to go deeper.

💡 Alternatives to GoHighLevel (GHL). GHL bundles CRM + calendar + SMS + automations in one place. Cheaper / more flexible substitutes: HubSpot, Close, or Keap; or a wired-together stack of Cal.com/Calendly (booking) + Zapier/Make (automation) + Brevo/Mailchimp (email) + Stripe. A stack costs less but you assemble it yourself.

Part 5: Tie down the booking — live, on the phone

This is the highest-leverage skill in the lesson. Do not hang up after they agree. Keep them on the line another 3–5 minutes and book them while you're still talking.

LIVE TIE-DOWN CHECKLIST (do all of these before hanging up)
1. "You got two or three more minutes? I just want to book you in
   so you actually get the email and know where to join."
2. Get their email — read it back, confirm it's correct.
3. Create the calendar event in your CRM while still on the call.
4. "Did you get that confirmation? Cool — go ahead and press YES on it."
5. If you have a "reply YES or it cancels" SMS automation, get the
   reply live too.
6. Optional: have them save your CRM number as a contact.
7. Verbal tie-down (humor works): "My manager said he'll beat my ass
   if someone no-shows — so, are you 100% sure you can make it?"
8. Book the call within the next 24–48 HOURS (see Part 7).

Two special cases: if they're driving, don't make them touch their phone — "just check your email as soon as you're parked and press yes to confirm, otherwise it gets cancelled." And follow up an hour later with a call or text if they haven't confirmed.

🔑 A booking that isn't tied down live is a coin-flip on show. The setter's job isn't "they agreed" — it's "they're confirmed."

Part 6: Objection handling — acknowledge → flip → reframe

Setting-call objections are surface-level (closing-call objections go much deeper). They almost always come from one of: lack of trust, ego, a past bad experience, no perceived value, defensiveness, or bad timing. The fundamental move is always the same:

ACKNOWLEDGE what they said  →  FLIP it back on them  →  REFRAME
"Yeah, totally, I get that"     a question that            in a way that
                                turns it around           reopens the call

Owen walks through 22 common objections. Reproducing all 22 as canned scripts would defeat the point — he refuses to hand them out as a resource precisely because "this won't work for every prospect. Everyone has different energy types." Treat the pattern as the skill and the lines below as illustrative starting points in Owen's voice — rewrite them in yours.

# Objection Root cause Illustrative response (adapt)
1 "I'm busy right now." Automatic brush-off "For sure — you got five minutes? If you don't, I probably couldn't help you anyway."
3 "How long will this take?" No perceived outcome "Five, ten minutes — do you have time for that?" then ask your first question immediately.
7 "Why you over the 10 others who called me?" Feels sold-to, wants truth "Honestly? That's a question I ask myself every single day." (humor + rapport)
8 "Just send me an email." No urgency, no trust "That's the point of the call — I don't even know yet if I can help you. Everyone's needs are different."
11 "Facebook ads don't work." Burned before "I get you — when's the last time you saw a Facebook ad? So do you think they work for anyone?"
14 "Just book me in." Brush-off; won't actually show "Honestly I don't know if we can even help you — we don't take just anyone with a pulse and a credit card, so let me learn a bit first."
16 "Tell me the price first." Distrust, wants to disqualify you "You know as well as me I won't drop a price on this call — and to be fair I don't know yet if we're even a fit." then a double-commitment question.
22 "I'm getting a phone call." Usually a lie to escape "Oh, perfect timing." (sarcastic) — if they keep talking, they weren't; if they hang up, they were.

The rest (e.g. "I don't need more work," "I've been burned before," "I'm driving," "I'm with a client," "are you reading a script?") follow the identical pattern: name the cause, acknowledge, flip, reframe — or, for genuine bad timing, simply set the next touch point and move on.

⚠️ Reality check on "we don't take anyone with a pulse and a credit card." Same rule as reverse qualification: it's a great line if it's true. If you'd take anyone who pays, don't say it. And note the compliance boundary — if it's a cold call, several of Owen's "just hang up" reflexes only exist because that person never consented; make sure your cold outreach was compliant in the first place (Lesson 22).

Part 7: Effortless execution — optimize three metrics

Everything ends in numbers. Diagnose the funnel front-to-back: pickup → booking → show.

Pickup rate (PUR) — do they answer?

Target ~75%; 3% means your number is burned. Levers:

  • Spam-protect your numbers with Wave (or another spam-protection app for your CRM numbers).
  • Check a number before you buy it — Google "phone number spam checker," paste it, pick a low-risk one.
  • Double-dial, never triple-dial. Two calls in a row is fine; a third in a row burns the number to "spam likely."
  • Match the area code to the prospect's region. Owen keeps 30–40 numbers across states; A2P registration stays with the sub-account, so buy more numbers and pick the right area code per day.

⚠️ Compliance boundary (from Lesson 22). Double-dialing and area-code matching are optimizations, not permission. They must stay inside TCPA, A2P 10DLC, and Do-Not-Call rules — scrub against the DNC registry, honor opt-outs, use registered sender IDs, and don't let "match the local area code" tip into deceptive caller-ID spoofing. The metric is not worth a violation.

Booking rate (ABR) — do they book?

Mostly a skill you build with reps. Two structural levers:

  • Speed to lead: contact an interested lead within 3–5 minutes. This alone "majorly improves your booking rate and pickup rate."
  • ~8 touchpoints. On average it takes about 8 contacts (cold SMS → call → confirmation → reminders → sales call → follow-ups) to convert. Work the leads you already have instead of always chasing new ones.

Show rate — do they turn up?

  • Lower the friction to join: use Google Meet (one click) over Zoom. (Get an upgraded workspace either way — free tiers cap meeting length.)
  • Make reminders human. Owen's signature automation, 43 minutes out: "good to hop on in 43?" — it reads like a person, not a robot, and lifts show rate.
  • Don't over-blast reminders — annoyed prospects no-show on purpose.
  • A strong pre-call VSL (video sales letter) pre-loads trust and testimonials so objections are handled before the call.
  • Book within 24–48 hours. Past ~40 hours, "they seem to just forget about it." Always book inside the next day or two.

💡 Tool alternatives. Wave (power dialer / spam protection) → Aircall, Kixie, PhoneBurner, JustCall, Orum. Send Blue (blue-bubble iMessage-style texting Owen mentions, ~$500/mo) → standard SMS via Twilio; note iMessage-for-business tools sit in a grey area, and Owen himself scaled to $20k/mo on plain CRM SMS, so this is a "later, if ever" purchase.

Part 8: See it live — the Bobby Dugas cold-SMS set

Owen shares a real cold-SMS set that later closed for $4k. Watching it, notice how the theory shows up naturally:

  • Opened with a double-commitment question without planning to: "So you're a roofing company owner, or just a general contractor?" — both answers lead toward the sale.
  • Built pain via numbers: got Bobby to admit he only does "six to nine roofs a year" and is "coming into a slow period."
  • Reverse-qualified with a real reason: "I don't really work with everybody… I only work with one person per area." — a true frame, not a bluff.
  • Made urgency and framing so strong the prospect asked to talk sooner.
  • Tied down live: got the email, read it back, booked it for noon the next day, confirmed the invite went out — and, because Bobby was driving, skipped the "reply yes" step per the driving rule.

🔑 The lesson of Bobby Dugas: reps teach the frameworks even before you know their names. Practice makes the moves automatic.

Part 9: Track everything

You cannot improve what you don't measure. Track pickup rate, booking rate (ABR), and show rate for every outbound method, and compare each to a KPI (target). Example: if cold-call ABR is 1% and the KPI is 5%, something in the funnel is off — track the sub-steps (pickup, pitch, resonation) and you'll usually find one thing out of whack. Fix it, ABR climbs. "Do not forget to track." — Owen.

Key takeaways

  1. Truthful numbers are the pain. On setting calls, make reality clear instead of going emotionally deep.
  2. "Why now?" is warm-lead only. Never ask it on cold outreach.
  3. Qualify against income / time / capacity, and reverse-qualify only when your standard is real.
  4. Tie down live or it's a coin-flip. Email confirmed, calendar "yes" pressed, booked within 24–48h, before you hang up.
  5. Objections = acknowledge → flip → reframe. The pattern is the skill; scripts are just illustrations.
  6. Three metrics run the game: pickup (~75%), booking (ABR), show — each with specific, compliant levers.

Common pitfalls

  • ❌ Hanging up the second they agree. The number-one show-rate killer. Stay on and tie it down.
  • ❌ Going closing-call deep on a setting call. Business owners resist emotional pain; use numbers.
  • ❌ Asking "why now?" on a cold call. It's illogical and it exposes you.
  • ❌ Triple-dialing or spoofing to chase pickup rate. You burn the number and risk a TCPA/DNC violation. Double-dial, stay compliant.
  • ❌ Using reverse qualification when you'd actually take anyone. That's manipulation; it churns clients and burns your reputation.
  • ❌ Booking three days out. After ~40 hours they forget. Book inside 24–48h.
  • ❌ Not tracking. Without pickup/ABR/show you're guessing which step is broken.

🛠️ Capstone Project: Your setting cheat-sheet + 10 logged reps

This is the main hands-on project for the lesson. You'll walk away with a one-page setting cheat-sheet you actually use on calls, plus baseline numbers for your three metrics.

What you will build

A single reference sheet tailored to your niche, and a small log of 10 reps that test it:

  • Qualification criteria for your niche (income / time / capacity cut-offs) — maps to Part 3.
  • A booking tie-down checklist — maps to Part 5.
  • Your top objection responses, rewritten in your voice — maps to Part 6.
  • A metrics log for 10 setting reps — maps to Parts 7 & 9.

Why this is the perfect practice

Lesson idea Where you use it in the cheat-sheet + reps
Truthful-numbers pain The "discovery" line on your sheet that surfaces real numbers
Qualification Your three niche cut-offs
Reverse qualification One honest "tactical skepticism" line you'd actually stand behind
Live tie-down The 8-step checklist you run before hanging up
Objection pattern Your acknowledge-flip-reframe responses to your top 5 objections
Metrics Pickup / ABR / show logged across 10 reps

Milestones (build them in order, each one works on its own)

  1. Write your qualification criteria. Fill in income, time, and capacity cut-offs for your niche in one line each. This alone is usable today.
  2. Draft the booking tie-down checklist. Copy Part 5's 8 steps and adapt the wording (including your driving-exception line).
  3. Write your top 5 objection responses. Pick the 5 objections you'll hear most; write each as acknowledge → flip → reframe in your own words. Mark them as yours, not Owen's.
  4. Add one honest reverse-qualification line. Only include it if the standard behind it is real for you.
  5. Set up your metrics log. Three columns — pickup, booked (Y/N), showed (Y/N) — one row per rep.
  6. Run 10 reps. Real dials if you're compliant and ready, otherwise mock calls with a partner (one plays the owner). Improve one thing each rep and note it.
  7. Stretch goals. Add a Google Meet + 43-minute human reminder to your CRM; check three CRM numbers through a spam checker before your next real dials; run 30 mock reps total (Owen's prescription) and finish Neuro-Selling.

How you will know you are done

  • ✅ A one-page cheat-sheet exists with all four sections filled in for your niche.
  • ✅ Every objection response is in your own voice and passes the "is this true?" test.
  • ✅ You've logged 10 reps and can state your pickup, booking, and show rate.
  • ✅ You've named the one weakest number and what you'll change next.

💡 Keep yourself honest: if any line on your sheet claims a standard you wouldn't actually enforce, cut it. A frame only persuades ethically when it's true.

Practice exercises (optional extra reps)

What these are: small, self-contained tasks, each giving focused practice on one idea. Optional and independent; the Capstone already touches all of them.

Exercise 1: Pain-in-numbers (foundational)

Take a mock prospect who "does 10–20 jobs a month." Write the 3 questions that would surface the real average — without exaggerating anything.

Exercise 2: Objection ladder (intermediate)

Pick 8 of the 22 objections. For each, write the root cause and your acknowledge-flip-reframe response. Read them aloud; cut any that sound robotic.

Exercise 3: Metric diagnosis (advanced)

Given a funnel — 100 dials, 20 pickups, 2 booked, 1 showed — compute pickup, ABR, and show rate. Name which single number you'd fix first and the specific, compliant lever you'd pull.
